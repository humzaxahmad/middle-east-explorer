title: "Russia's gas exports to Europe collapse"
speed: fast
trigger: "Russia's pipeline gas exports to Europe were already falling after the September 2022 Nord Stream sabotage, which destroyed three of the four lines that had carried 17% of the EU's gas imports. The last major route closed on 1 January 2025, when Ukraine let its five-decade-old transit agreement with Gazprom expire rather than keep funding Russia's war effort, cutting off the remaining significant pipeline gas to Slovakia, Austria and Hungary."
energyHit: "Russian pipeline exports to Europe have collapsed from 152.4 bcm in 2021 to about 18 bcm in 2025, their lowest level since the 1970s, with the remaining volume running through a single pipeline, TurkStream, into Hungary, Slovakia and Serbia. Russia's share of EU gas imports fell from 45% in 2021 to roughly 12% in 2025. Europe filled the gap mostly with LNG: European LNG imports rose 29% in 2025, and the EU commissioned 12 new LNG import terminals between 2022 and 2024, adding 70 bcm of import capacity. Qatar became the EU's fourth-largest LNG supplier, though only about 12% of its LNG goes to Europe against roughly 85% to Asia, and Qatar's own exports were separately disrupted in 2026 by the Strait of Hormuz closure, a reminder that even a winner from this shock can be exposed to a different one."
economyHit: "European gas prices remain far below the roughly EUR350/MWh peak of the 2022 crisis, but the TTF benchmark still touched EUR100/MWh in late October 2026, the first time since 2022, more than double pre-war levels. The bigger economic story is demand destruction rather than a price spike: European gas demand is down about 20% from pre-2022 levels, as energy-intensive industry permanently cut back or relocated rather than absorb the higher cost, a structural loss of competitiveness rather than a one-off shock."
sectorsWinners:
  - "Qatar and the US, which filled much of the volume Russia used to supply, though Qatar's own gains were later squeezed by the Hormuz closure"
  - "LNG shipping and terminal operators, who built and now run the EU's new import infrastructure"
  - "Non-Russian gas producers generally, selling into a tighter, higher-priced European market"
sectorsLosers:
  - "European heavy industry and chemicals, which lost access to cheap Russian pipeline gas permanently and in some cases cut output or relocated rather than absorb the cost"
  - "Russia's own state budget, which lost a reliable, high-volume customer it has only partly replaced by selling more to China"
  - "European households, who still pay more for gas and electricity than before 2022"
countriesExposed: ["RUS"]
countriesProtected: ["QAT"]
whatToWatch: "Whether Russia and China's planned second pipeline, Power of Siberia 2, gets built, giving Russia a much larger outlet to replace lost European revenue; European gas demand and TTF price trends each winter, the clearest read on whether the EU's diversification is holding; and whether Qatar's own Hormuz-related export problems ease enough for it to keep growing as a European supplier, or whether the US becomes the larger beneficiary instead."
interviewAngle: "The lesson isn't \"Russia lost, Qatar won.\" Qatar sends only about 12% of its LNG to Europe, and in 2026 its own exports were hit by a completely different chokepoint, Hormuz. The real winner from Russia's gas losses was LNG infrastructure and diversification generally, not any single supplier."
summary: "Russia's pipeline gas exports to Europe collapsed from 152.4 bcm in 2021 to about 18 bcm in 2025, first from the 2022 Nord Stream sabotage and then from Ukraine's refusal to renew its gas transit deal in January 2025. The EU filled the gap mainly with LNG, building 12 new import terminals and turning to Qatar and the US, though European gas demand is still down 20% from pre-2022 levels as energy-intensive industry absorbed a permanently higher cost of energy."
