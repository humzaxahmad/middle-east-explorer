energyHit: "Shipping through the Suez Canal and Bab el-Mandeb, the route for most Asia-Europe trade and a meaningful share of Gulf oil, has collapsed repeatedly since 2023. Roughly 65% of container traffic that once used Suez now sails around the Cape of Good Hope instead, adding 10 to 15 days and about 3,000 nautical miles to a typical Asia-Europe voyage. War-risk insurance for the route ran as high as 3% of a ship's value in 2026, against roughly 0.25% before the crisis, and container freight rates hit $7,000 to $13,000 per container in July 2026. The August 2026 strikes near Yanbu matter beyond Yemen's coast: Yanbu is the Red Sea terminus of Saudi Arabia's main pipeline bypass around the Strait of Hormuz, so the same Houthi campaign threatens oil that has already found its way around one chokepoint."
economyHit: "Egypt, which depends on Suez Canal tolls for a large share of its foreign currency earnings, has lost an estimated $10 billion in canal revenue since the crisis began, with monthly losses running as high as $800 million at the worst of the disruption; canal revenue only partly recovered, to $4.67 billion for the 2025/26 fiscal year, still well below the roughly $10 billion a year it earned before 2023. The longer transit times and higher freight and insurance costs add to the same inflation pressure coming from the separate Strait of Hormuz closure, squeezing European and Asian importers' supply chains at the same time as their energy bills rise."
